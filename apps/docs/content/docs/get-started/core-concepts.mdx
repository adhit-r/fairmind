## AI system

The thing being governed: a model, an agent, or an application built on them. It belongs to an organisation, has an owner and a risk tier, and names the frameworks that apply. Everything else hangs off the system. See [System context](/docs/get-started/system-context).

## Framework, requirement and control

A **framework** is a body of rules, such as the EU AI Act or ISO/IEC 42001. It breaks into **requirements** (a clause or article). FairMind maps requirements to **controls**: the things an organisation actually does or tests. One control can serve several frameworks.

## Pack and workflow

A **pack** is what you install for a framework: the **workflows** that produce evidence for its requirements. A workflow is a repeatable sequence of steps: collect, check, ask a person, file evidence. A person starts every run; the agent can draft a step when asked.

## Evaluation

A test with a stated method and threshold. FairMind runs an evaluation in one of three ways:

| Mode | Input | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upload | Files you provide | A predictions CSV for fairness metrics |
| Live | The system's connected endpoint, judged by your organisation's model | Prompt-injection and jailbreak behaviour tests |
| Connection | Your agent platform's configuration, read-only | Tool permissions and memory settings of managed agents |

61 of the 109 catalogue evaluations run in FairMind. The rest accept evidence from your own tools.

## Evidence

The record a decision rests on: what was tested, how, the raw result, its provenance and limits, and who reviewed it. Evidence has a strength: **measured** (FairMind ran it) counts for more than **attested** (a person stated it). See [Evidence model](/docs/get-started/evidence-model).

## Release gate

Five checks: fairness, data governance, human oversight, monitoring and environmental. Each reads the newest evidence on file:

- **Proven**: measured or tool-estimated, with confidence of 0.70 or more.
- **Attested**: anything else on file, such as a person's statement. It counts for little.
- **Open**: nothing on file, or a monitoring snapshot older than one and a half cadences.

The verdict comes from the system's risk tier and the overall confidence across the five checks: `go`, `conditional_go` or `no_go`. A measured fairness failure never passes as a plain `go`, and failing blocking policy rules force `no_go`.

## Decision

A person approves or rejects a release request. The gate informs the decision; it never makes it. A rejection makes the verdict `no_go` until a reviewer approves a new request, and approval is refused while critical findings are open.
