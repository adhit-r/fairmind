FairMind is an AI assurance evidence workbench. It keeps an AI system, its tests, evidence, risks, remediation, approvals, and reports in one reviewable context.

The product is built around a simple rule: an evaluator result is evidence for a decision, not the decision itself.

## What FairMind helps you do

1. Register an AI system and define its governance scope.
2. Run a supported evaluator or bring an external result.
3. Record artifacts with provenance, limitations, and review state.
4. Link evidence to risks and governance controls.
5. Assign remediation, run a retest, and preserve the history.
6. Create a point-in-time assurance snapshot for review.

## The core lifecycle

| Stage | Question | Primary workspace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onboard | What system are we governing? | System registration |
| Assess | What was tested, with which method and threshold? | Test Lab |
| Govern | What evidence and risks affect the decision? | Evidence & Controls |
| Remediate | What must change, who owns it, and was it retested? | Remediation |
| Operate | What changed after release? | Monitoring and reports |

## Evidence, not automatic compliance

<Callout type="warn" title="Human review is required">
FairMind records technical results and governance decisions. It does not turn an evaluation, a control mapping, or a readiness score into certification, legal compliance, or approval.
</Callout>

The product makes capability state visible because different surfaces prove different things. Environmental evaluation is the strongest evidence-producing vertical in the current release. Other evaluators may be external-provider dependent, metadata-only, interface-only, unavailable, or research-only.

Continue with [Capability truth](/docs/concepts/capability-truth) before selecting an evaluator.

## Who uses FairMind

- Assurance and compliance leads review gaps, mappings, approvals, and snapshots.
- ML engineers and evaluators register subjects, run supported tests, and attach artifacts.
- Risk and system owners manage remediation, exceptions, and release blockers.
- Auditors and reviewers inspect evidence, decisions, and audit history.
- Organization administrators manage membership and access.
