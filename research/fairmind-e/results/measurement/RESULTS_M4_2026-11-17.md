# Power-Reference Calibration, 2026-11-17: Apple M4 Mac mini

Second calibration attempt, and the first usable one. The August attempt on the
M1 Pro was discarded for background load (`CALIBRATION_ATTEMPT_2026-08-01.md`).
This run used a dedicated, quiet host.

Artifacts: `m4_runs_ref.csv`, `m4_runs_ref_mitigations.csv`,
`m4_power_reference.csv`, `m4_calibration.json`, workload sizes in
`../../measurement/overrides_m4.json`. The raw `powermetrics` log (400 MB, 90,204
samples over 18,671 s; 21 MB gzipped) and the 2-minute idle log are kept outside
git and belong in the next release archive.

## Host and protocol

- Apple M4 Mac mini, 16 GB, macOS 26.3.1, mains power, no other applications open.
- Python 3.13, torch 2.15.0, codecarbon 3.3.2 (from `uv.lock`).
- `HOST_TAG=m4 OVERRIDES=overrides_m4.json bash run_with_power_reference.sh`:
  four workloads by five iterations, 20 s idle gaps, `powermetrics` at 200 ms,
  then five repeats of the mitigation suite. 23:16 to 04:26 IST.
- Idle baseline: mean 0.575 W, median 0.009 W. Run-local gap medians were used
  for background (0.009 W for 18 of 20 runs).
- The M1 sizes ran under 60 s per iteration on the M4, too short for
  codecarbon's 15 s sampling, so `overrides_m4.json` rescales them (median
  durations 79.9, 62.4, 85.7, 80.6 s). W2 is held at ~62 s because the
  bfloat16 rerun costs ~49 min per repeat.

## Calibration (main suite, 19 of 20 runs)

| Method | Class | Confidence | Median abs. error | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| codecarbon (cpu_load fallback) | manual | 0.30 | 106% | over |
| manual FLOP | manual | 0.20 | 203% | over |

Spearman rho between assigned confidence and error: -0.63 (n = 38).

The absolute errors above inherit the FLOP model's placeholder constant, so
they cannot rank the methods on their own (raised in the mock review of
2026-11-17). `median_abs_pct_error_after_median_rescale` removes each method's
own median ratio first: 6.79% for codecarbon against 39.35% for the FLOP
model. The tracker still tracks the reference far more closely, with the
constant out of the picture. Both are `manual`, so this is evidence within the
lowest class, not for the class hierarchy. One run (`batch_embeddings real-i05`) is excluded as
contaminated at 62%, but this is an artifact: the idle gap after the last
main-suite run overlaps the start of the mitigation suite, so its "background"
is workload. Its total reference energy matches its four siblings.

## Readings

1. **The degraded tracker is a clock on a second chip.** It implies a flat
   11.8-12.7 W on every workload while measured draw ranges 5.4-11.5 W. On the
   M1 it implied a flat 23.5-27.2 W. Classing it `manual` is supported.
2. **The FLOP estimate's error is workload-dependent**: 1.9x to 15.9x against
   the reference, an 8x spread.
3. **Scope caveat.** `powermetrics` reports CPU + GPU + ANE from Apple's own
   model and excludes DRAM, storage, networking and PSU losses; codecarbon adds
   a RAM estimate. Part of the over-estimate is therefore a scope mismatch, not
   tracker error. The ordering result does not depend on it; absolute error
   figures should not be quoted without this caveat. A wall-power meter would
   settle it.
4. **Only the bottom of the hierarchy is tested.** Both methods are `manual` on
   Apple silicon. `measured` vs `tool_estimated` vs `vendor_reported` needs a
   host where codecarbon reads hardware counters (Linux RAPL or an NVIDIA GPU).

## Mitigations (five repeats; tracker energy, no power reference)

The mitigation CSV records no start/end times, so these runs are not matched to
the power log; the energies are codecarbon's.

| Mitigation | M4, median of 5 (range) | M1, single run | Replicates |
|---|---|---|---|
| bfloat16 fine-tune | +4071% (+3769..+4140), 44x slower | +3976%, 46x slower | yes |
| RAG float64 -> float32 | -15.9% (-18.2..-15.2) | -17.5% | yes |
| batching, embeddings | -1.3% (-2.7..+0.3) | -22.8% | **no** |

- The M4 implements the Arm BF16 extension (`hw.optional.arm.FEAT_BF16: 1`;
  the M1 Pro reports 0), yet the cast is still 44x slower. The slowdown is the
  PyTorch CPU bfloat16 path on this platform, not missing hardware support.
- The M1 batching saving was a single execution and does not reproduce over
  five repeats on the M4. Treat it as host-dependent, not as a saving.
