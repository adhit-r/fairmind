# M5 Measurement Study: Results (Apple M1 Pro, 2026-07-31)

Study of record for the CCAI workshop submission. Four workloads, five
iterations, three measurement methods, executed on real hardware.

- Data: `m1_runs.csv` (60 rows), mitigation pairs in `m1_runs_mitigations.csv`
- Workload scaling: `../../measurement/overrides_m1.json`
- Instrument validation: `../../measurement/PILOT_2026-07-31.md`
- Gate replay: `../../analysis/gate_from_measurements.py`

## Headline: measurement provenance decided the release, at identical impact

Replaying every available run through the **production decision engine**
(`src.domain.environmental`, 40 rows, zero errors):

| Method | Provenance | Confidence | Band | Impact tier | Decision | n |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| codecarbon | tool_estimated | 0.72 | measured | low | **go** | 20 |
| manual_flop | manual | 0.30 | unknown | low | **conditional_go** | 20 |

**The release decision changed on 20 of 20 paired runs.** The impact tier was
`low` in every case, so nothing about the magnitude drove the difference: the
same physical run, measured two ways, was released unconditionally under one
method and held for mitigation under the other, purely because of how the
number was obtained.

This is the quantity a magnitude-only gate cannot express. A carbon-budget
gate or a readiness-level gate sees `low` impact in both columns and passes
both.

## Method agreement

The two methods disagree substantially on the magnitude itself, which is the
second reason evidence quality matters.

| Direction | MAPE |
| --- | ---: |
| codecarbon as reference | 122.6 percent |
| manual_flop as reference | 169.1 percent |

Per workload (codecarbon as reference): batch_embeddings 58.2, classical ML
81.8, RAG 52.5, transformer fine-tune 297.8 percent.

Bland-Altman, energy in kWh: mean bias 3.19e-04, SD of differences 7.50e-04,
95 percent limits of agreement -1.15e-03 to 1.79e-03.

`cloud_billing` produced no rows: 20 of 60 records are honest
`method_available=false` entries with a stated reason, because real invoices
lag execution by hours to days. Nothing was imputed.

## H4: not supported, and the reason is the interesting part

Pre-registered test, paired t over 11 mitigation pairs across 4 workloads,
intensity in kgCO2e (location-based) per run:

| Statistic | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| t (df 10) | -0.859 |
| p | 0.410 |
| mean reduction | -311.2 percent |
| median reduction | 33.9 percent |
| 95 percent CI on mean reduction | -1051.3 to 70.8 percent |
| target (>20 percent) met | no, on point estimate and on CI lower bound |

**H4 is not supported by this data.** Reporting only the median, or dropping
the outlier, would be cherry-picking, so the pre-registered result stands as
computed.

The diagnostic matters more than the verdict. Ten of eleven pairs did reduce
intensity: region shift 94.2 percent on all four workloads, time shift 33.9
percent on all four, batching 22.8 percent, and quantisation 17.5 percent on
RAG. A single pair inverted the mean:

| Pair | Pre | Post | Change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| quantisation, transformer fine-tune | 2.680e-04 kgCO2e | 1.092e-02 kgCO2e | **-3976 percent** |

Cause, from the measured durations: switching the fine-tune from `float32` to
`bfloat16` took the run from **62.7 s to 2887.6 s, about 46 times slower**.
PyTorch has no optimised bfloat16 CPU kernel on this ARM host, so the
reduced-precision path fell back to slow emulation and burned roughly forty
times the energy.

This is a real result, not an instrumentation fault, and it is the mitigation
analogue of the paper's central argument. "We quantised the model, therefore
we cut carbon" is a routine and entirely plausible claim. Measured on the
actual deployment hardware it was false by more than an order of magnitude,
in the harmful direction. A governance gate that accepts asserted mitigations
would have recorded a reduction that never happened, which is precisely why
ENV-6 requires the mitigation to be documented and re-measured rather than
declared.

## Three limitations that bound what this data can claim

1. **No hardware power counters.** Apple Silicon exposes no RAPL and
   `powermetrics` needs root, so codecarbon fell back to CPU-load estimation
   against a TDP constant. Its figures are honestly `tool_estimated`, but this
   study therefore compares an estimate against an estimate, never against a
   hardware reading, and the `measured` provenance class is unclaimed
   throughout. A RAPL-capable Linux host is required before the COMPASS full
   paper.

2. **Only the low-impact row of the matrix is exercised.** These are benchmark
   workloads: the largest run emitted about 2e-03 kgCO2e, some five orders of
   magnitude below the engine's provisional `low` ceiling for training
   (100 kgCO2e). The medium and high rows of the decision matrix are not
   touched by this data, and **the provisional impact thresholds cannot be
   recalibrated from it**. Recalibration needs production-scale training runs,
   not a laptop benchmark. The provisional markers in `impact.py` stay.

3. **The manual FLOP model rests on placeholder constants.** It assumes
   4 GFLOP/s per watt and PUE 1.0, both marked PLACEHOLDER, and the grid table
   is a placeholder edition. The MAPE figures above therefore measure the gap
   between a load-model tool and a crudely parameterised analytic estimate,
   which is a real and practically relevant comparison, but not a calibrated
   error bound.

## A methodological catch worth recording

The measurement harness writes its own gate columns (`recommendation`,
`risk_tier`, `impact_level`) using the smoke module's helper functions and its
own kilogram thresholds (`IMPACT_LOW_MAX_KG = 0.5`), which are **not** the
production engine or its per-phase thresholds. Citing those columns would mean
reporting a reimplementation rather than the shipped gate.

`analysis/gate_from_measurements.py` was written to close this: it replays the
measurement rows through `src.domain.environmental.run_assessment` and reports
the production decision. On this dataset the two happened to agree on all 40
rows, so no conclusion changes, but **the engine replay is the citable result**
and the harness gate columns should be treated as provisional bookkeeping.

## Reproduce

```bash
cd apps/backend
uv run python ../../research/fairmind-e/measurement/harness.py \
    --workload all --iterations 5 --region IND --seed 1337 \
    --config-overrides ../../research/fairmind-e/measurement/overrides_m1.json \
    --mitigations \
    --output ../../research/fairmind-e/results/measurement/m1_runs.csv

uv run python ../../research/fairmind-e/analysis/gate_from_measurements.py \
    --runs ../../research/fairmind-e/results/measurement/m1_runs.csv

uv run python ../../research/fairmind-e/analysis/hypotheses.py \
    --runs ../../research/fairmind-e/results/measurement/m1_runs.csv \
    --mitigation ../../research/fairmind-e/results/measurement/m1_runs_mitigations.csv
```

Host: Apple M1 Pro, arm64, macOS. codecarbon 3.2.9, scikit-learn 1.9.0,
numpy 2.4.6, torch 2.13.0, Python 3.13.
