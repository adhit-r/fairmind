# M5 Pilot: Instrumentation Validation

Date: 2026-07-31
Host: Apple M1 Pro (arm64), macOS
Environment: backend venv, Python 3.13, codecarbon 3.2.9, scikit-learn 1.9.0,
numpy 2.4.6, torch absent

Purpose: confirm the real measurement path produces genuine data end to end
before committing a full day to the five-iteration study. This is a pilot of
the instrument, not study data, and none of its numbers belong in the paper.

## What ran

One real iteration of W1 (classical ML training), region IND, all three
measurement methods attempted.

## Result: the gate flips on provenance alone

| Method | Available | Energy (kWh) | Location kgCO2e | Provenance | Confidence | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| codecarbon | yes | 6.52403e-06 | 4.65451e-06 | tool_estimated | 0.75 | go |
| cloud_billing | no | - | - | unknown | 0.00 | - |
| manual_flop | yes | 1.33333e-06 | 9.50667e-07 | manual | 0.20 | conditional_go |

The same physical run, at identical true impact, produced **different release
recommendations depending only on how it was measured**. That is the paper's
central claim reproduced on real data rather than fixtures, and it is the
quantity a magnitude-only gate cannot express.

The two energy figures also differ by roughly a factor of five, which is the
raw material for the MAPE and Bland-Altman agreement statistics once the full
study runs.

`cloud_billing` correctly recorded itself unavailable with a reason rather
than inventing a figure, which is the honesty rule working as intended.

## Two conditions to satisfy before the study of record

1. **No hardware power counters on this host.** codecarbon logged `No CPU
   tracking mode found. Falling back on CPU load mode`: Apple Silicon exposes
   no RAPL, and `powermetrics` requires root. The resulting figure is a load
   model, not a hardware reading. It remains honestly `tool_estimated`, but a
   study run here compares an estimate against an estimate, and the
   `measured` provenance class stays unclaimed. Prefer a RAPL-capable Linux
   host, or disclose this in the limitations.

2. **W1 completes in 0.93 s**, far below codecarbon's 15 s sampling window in
   real mode, so its energy figure is extrapolated from almost no samples.
   Workload configs must be scaled up (via `--config-overrides`) until each
   iteration spans several sampling intervals. See RUNBOOK section 1b.

W2 (transformer fine-tune) was unavailable because torch is not installed;
the harness recorded that honestly and continued with the other workloads.

## Operator decisions taken (2026-07-31)

**Host: this Apple M1 Pro, with the limitation disclosed.** A RAPL-capable
Linux machine was not available, and holding the study for hardware that
cannot be provisioned costs more than the caveat does. The M1 run still
yields a real dataset and reproduces the provenance-flip result. Consequence
for the paper: the `measured` provenance class stays unclaimed, and the
agreement statistics compare a tool's load model against an analytic
estimate. Recommendation: re-run on a RAPL-capable Linux host before the
COMPASS full paper; the CCAI workshop submission can carry this dataset with
the limitation stated.

**Workloads scaled to roughly 80 s per iteration.** Measured at the default
`REAL_CONFIG` on this host:

| Workload | Default duration | Scaling applied | Target |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| classical_ml_training | 1.98 s | `repeats` 3 -> 120 | ~79 s |
| transformer_finetune | 11.98 s | `steps` 400 -> 2600 | ~78 s |
| rag_inference | 60.46 s | `repeats` 5 -> 7 | ~85 s |
| batch_embeddings | 12.35 s | `repeats` 5 -> 32 | ~79 s |

Only the linear repeat knobs were touched, so the analytic FLOP estimate
scales with the real work and the two methods stay comparable. The override
file is `overrides_m1.json`, archived beside the results as the runbook
requires.

**torch installed**, so W2 is available and the study covers all four
workloads rather than three.
